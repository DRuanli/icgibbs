\begin{abstract}
% Every number comes from numbers.tex / numbers_v2.tex. Claims: matrix rows 2, 3, 6, 8, 13, 14, 15, 16, 18.
Tabular foundation models (TFMs) predict labels in context without training, but they need labelled examples. We show
that a frozen TFM can learn from weak labels, such as candidate sets or label proportions within bags, without updating
its weights. Under exchangeability, the distribution of one unknown label given all the others is a posterior
predictive with the other points as context, which is what a TFM computes. \ours{} therefore samples the latent labels
with forward passes, brings in the weak labels through their likelihood, and predicts by averaging over the samples.
Its exact variants target the Bayes posterior when the TFM is exact. For the sampler as run, we show that under a
homogeneity assumption on the TFM a one-dimensional response map bounds where a chain can go, so that with two
attractors the start matters. Three pre-registered studies test the method. On \val{s2-n-total} new datasets a single
chain with TabICL~v2 outperformed specialised learners for label proportions (\val{s2-llp-cmp-diff} AUC over the
registered comparator, \val{s2-llp-strongest-diff} over the strongest baseline) and, narrowly, for partial labels;
with TabPFN~v2 the same chain had fallen short under heavy ambiguity. On \val{s3-n-total} further datasets with four
TFMs, averaging chains from several starts, one of them a tuned specialised learner (\val{s3-h1-mean},
$p\val{s3-h1-prel}$; median \val{s3-h1-median}), and then over TFMs (\val{s3-h2-mean}, $p\val{s3-h2-prel}$) improved on
the single chain and matched the best TFM of each dataset. Two registered criteria failed: a label-free diagnostic of
stalling, and a limit on how often the multi-start average of one TFM falls behind the better of its single chain and
the tuned learner.
\end{abstract}
