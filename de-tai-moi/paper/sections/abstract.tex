\begin{abstract}
Tabular foundation models (TFMs) predict labels in context without training, but they need labelled examples. We show
that a frozen TFM can learn from weak labels, such as candidate sets or label proportions within bags, without updating
its weights. Under exchangeability, the distribution of one unknown label given all the others is a posterior
predictive with the other points as context, which is what a TFM computes. \ours{} therefore samples the latent labels
with forward passes, brings in the weak labels through their likelihood, and predicts by averaging over the samples.
Its exact variants target the Bayes posterior when the TFM is exact. For the sampler as run, a homogeneity assumption
on the TFM gives floors and ceilings for the label accuracy of a chain, so that where a chain starts can matter. Three
pre-registered studies test the method. On \val{s2-n-total} new datasets a single chain with TabICL~v2 outperformed
specialised learners for label proportions (\val{s2-llp-cmp-diff} AUC over the registered comparator) and, narrowly,
for partial labels; with TabPFN~v2 the same chain had fallen short under heavy ambiguity. On \val{s3-n-total} further
datasets with four TFMs, averaging chains from several starts, one of them a tuned specialised learner, improved on the
single chain (\val{s3-h1-mean}, one-sided $p\val{s3-h1-prel}$; median \val{s3-h1-median}), and averaging over TFMs
improved on a single TFM (\val{s3-h2-mean}, $p\val{s3-h2-prel}$). Two registered criteria failed, and with one TFM the
multi-start average was not significantly better than the tuned learner. In a supplementary analysis registered after
Study~3 was scored, the multi-TFM average beat untuned tree-based self-training on \val{s3b-x-gbdt-win} of
\val{s3b-x-gbdt-n} datasets and partial-label $k$NN on all \val{s3b-x-plknn-n}; a single chain was not clearly better
than the tree-based learner, and running chains for 100 instead of 30 sweeps rarely repaired a stalled chain.
\end{abstract}
