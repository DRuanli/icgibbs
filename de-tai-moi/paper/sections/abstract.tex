\begin{abstract}
Tabular foundation models (TFMs) predict labels in context without training, but they need labelled examples. We show
that a frozen TFM can learn from weak labels such as candidate sets or label proportions. Under
exchangeability, the distribution of one unknown label given all the others is a posterior predictive, which is what a
TFM approximates. \ours{} therefore samples the latent labels with forward passes, brings in the weak labels
through their likelihood, and predicts by averaging over the samples. Its exact variants target the Bayes posterior
when the TFM is exact, and a model of the TFM's response to noisy context labels explains why the start of a chain can
matter. Three pre-registered studies test the method. On \val{s2-n-total} new datasets a single chain with TabICL~v2
outperformed specialised learners for label proportions (\val{s2-llp-cmp-diff} AUC over the registered comparator)
and for partial labels (\val{s2-pll3-cmp-diff} accuracy). On \val{s3-n-total} further datasets with four TFMs, averaging
chains from several starts improved on a single chain, and averaging over TFMs improved on a TFM drawn at random and
matched the best TFM of each dataset. The multi-TFM average also beat untuned tree-based self-training and
partial-label nearest neighbours on every dataset of their settings.
\end{abstract}
