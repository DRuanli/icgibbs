\begin{abstract}
% Every number comes from numbers.tex / numbers_v2.tex. Claims: matrix rows 2, 3, 6, 8, 13, 14, 15, 16, 18.
Tabular foundation models (TFMs) predict labels in context without training, but they need labelled examples. We show
that a frozen TFM can learn from weak labels, such as candidate sets or label proportions within bags, without updating
a weight. Under exchangeability, the distribution of one unknown label given all the others is a posterior predictive
with the other points as context, which is what a TFM computes. \ours{} therefore samples the latent labels with forward
passes, brings in the weak labels through their likelihood, and predicts by averaging over the samples. We prove that
its exact variants target the Bayes posterior when the TFM is exact and, for the sampler as run with a real TFM, that a
one-dimensional response map of the TFM governs where a chain ends: with two attractors, the start decides. Three
pre-registered studies test the method. On \val{s2-n-total} new datasets, a single chain with TabICL~v2 outperformed
the strongest specialised learners for label proportions (\val{s2-llp-cmp-diff} AUC over DLLP) and partial labels; with
TabPFN~v2 the same chain stalled under heavy ambiguity, which the response map explains. On \val{s3-n-total} further
datasets with four TFMs, averaging chains from several starts (\val{s3-h1-mean}, $p\val{s3-h1-prel}$) and then over
TFMs (\val{s3-h2-mean}, $p\val{s3-h2-prel}$) improved on the single chain and matched the best TFM of each dataset. Two
registered criteria failed: a label-free diagnostic of stalling, and a bound on how often one TFM falls behind a tuned
specialised learner.
\end{abstract}
