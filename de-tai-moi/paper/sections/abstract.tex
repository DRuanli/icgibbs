\begin{abstract}
Tabular foundation models (TFMs) predict labels in context without training, but they need labelled examples. We show
that a frozen TFM can learn from weak labels, such as candidate sets or label proportions within bags, without updating
its weights. Under exchangeability, the distribution of one unknown label given all the others is a posterior
predictive with the other points as context, which is what a TFM approximates. \ours{} therefore samples the latent
labels with forward passes, brings in the weak labels through their likelihood, and predicts by averaging over the
samples. Its exact variants target the Bayes posterior when the TFM is exact. For the default sampler on instance-level
weak labels with unsubsampled contexts, a homogeneity assumption on the TFM gives floors and ceilings for the label
accuracy of a chain that hold with high probability; they show why the start of a chain can matter. Three pre-registered studies test the method. The
first, with TabPFN~v2, showed large gains over the naive use of the TFM and that the method depends on how the TFM
handles noisy context labels. On \val{s2-n-total} new datasets a single chain with TabICL~v2 outperformed specialised
neural and linear learners for label proportions (\val{s2-llp-cmp-diff} AUC over the registered comparator, linear DLLP;
\val{s2-llp-strongest-diff} over the strongest baseline) and for partial labels (\val{s2-pll3-cmp-diff} accuracy over
tuned CC at both ambiguity levels). On \val{s3-n-total} further datasets with four TFMs, averaging chains from several starts, one of them a
tuned specialised learner, improved on the single chain (\val{s3-h1-mean} in pooled AUC and accuracy, one-sided
$p\val{s3-h1-prel}$), and averaging over TFMs improved on a TFM drawn at random (\val{s3-h2-mean}, $p\val{s3-h2-prel}$)
and matched the best TFM of each dataset without knowing which it is. In a supplementary analysis, the multi-TFM average
beat untuned tree-based self-training on all \val{s3x-x-gbdt-llp-n} label-proportion datasets (median
\val{s3x-x-gbdt-llp-median} AUC) and partial-label $k$NN on all \val{s3b-x-plknn-n} partial-label datasets, and chains
run for 100 instead of 30 sweeps kept improving (\val{s3b-b3-gain} accuracy).
\end{abstract}
