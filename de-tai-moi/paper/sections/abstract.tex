\begin{abstract}
Tabular foundation models (TFMs) predict labels in context without training, but they need labelled examples. We show
that a frozen TFM can learn from weak labels, such as candidate sets or label proportions within bags, without updating
its weights. Under exchangeability, the distribution of one unknown label given all the others is a posterior
predictive with the other points as context, which is what a TFM approximates. \ours{} therefore samples the latent
labels with forward passes, brings in the weak labels through their likelihood, and predicts by averaging over the
samples. Its exact variants target the Bayes posterior when the TFM is exact. For the default sampler on instance-level
weak labels with unsubsampled contexts, a homogeneity assumption on the TFM gives floors and ceilings for the label
accuracy of a chain that hold with high probability; they show qualitatively that where a chain starts can matter, but
certify nothing at our sample sizes. Three pre-registered studies test the method. On \val{s2-n-total} new datasets a
single chain with TabICL~v2 outperformed specialised neural and linear learners for label proportions (\val{s2-llp-cmp-diff} AUC
over the registered comparator, linear DLLP; \val{s2-llp-strongest-diff} over the strongest baseline) and, narrowly,
for partial labels (one test did not survive a second chain); with TabPFN~v2 the same chain had fallen short under
heavy ambiguity. On \val{s3-n-total} further datasets with four TFMs, averaging chains from several starts, one of them
a tuned specialised learner, improved on the single chain (\val{s3-h1-mean} in pooled AUC and accuracy, one-sided
$p\val{s3-h1-prel}$; median \val{s3-h1-median}), and averaging over TFMs improved on the expected score of a TFM drawn
at random (\val{s3-h2-mean}, $p\val{s3-h2-prel}$) but not significantly on TabICL~v2 alone (exploratory). Two
registered criteria failed, and with one TFM the multi-start average was not significantly better than the tuned
learner. In a supplementary analysis registered after Study~3 was scored, and so designed with its results known, the
multi-TFM average beat untuned tree-based self-training on all \val{s3x-x-gbdt-llp-n} label-proportion datasets
(median \val{s3x-x-gbdt-llp-median} AUC) and partial-label $k$NN on all \val{s3b-x-plknn-n} partial-label datasets; a
single chain was level with the tree-based learner on label proportions; and running chains for 100 instead of 30
sweeps repaired only \val{s3b-b3-rep} of \val{s3b-b3-stuck} stalled chains, although, against our registered
prediction, it raised accuracy on average (\val{s3b-b3-gain}, mostly with TabPFN-3).
\end{abstract}
