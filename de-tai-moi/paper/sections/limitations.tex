\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}
\textbf{What each study supports.} The comparison with specialised baselines is confirmatory only for the single chain
with TabICL~v2 (Study~2), against neural or linear learners, and on PLL its margin is small. Study~3 has
\val{s3-n-total} datasets (\val{s3-n-multi} multiclass) and registered one baseline, the tuned learner $L$; its
comparison with $L$ is exploratory, and H2$''$ compared the multi-TFM average with a TFM drawn at random, not with the
best fixed TFM. The non-neural baselines of \cref{sec:s3b} were registered after Study~3 was scored and run untuned, the
tree-based learner was nearly degenerate on several partial-label datasets, and we ran no ensemble of $L$; tuned or
stronger non-neural learners might close part of the gap. The typical gain of several starts is small (median
\val{s3-h1-median}) and came from the learner start, which needs a trained learner. Pre-registration rests on commit
times that we control (\cref{app:prereg}).
\textbf{Failed criteria and predictions.} We have no label-free diagnostic of stalling that transfers across TFMs; with
one TFM the multi-start sampler was more than 0.02 behind the tuned learner in \val{s3-c4-vs-L} of \val{s3-n-pairs}
pairs; and our prediction that longer chains would barely improve (P22) was false.
\textbf{Theory versus practice.} The exactness results do not cover the uncorrected blocks used by default.
\Cref{thm:basins} covers the default sampler only for instance-level weak labels and contexts of at most 2{,}000
points (not LLP or subsampled contexts), rests on an assumption whose tolerance we could only bound from below (about
\val{th-dlb-med} in a typical cell), certifies nothing at our sample sizes, and does not describe slow mixing, which
longer chains showed to be part of what stalls a chain.
\textbf{Scope and cost.} Weak labels are synthetic except for three PLL datasets, and TFM developers may have used some
of our datasets (we excluded only TabDPT's pretraining sets). The claims cover LLP with random bags of 8 and PLL with
uniform distractors at $q\le0.6$. For PU, label noise and semi-supervised learning, where the naive labels lose no
information, the sampler was worse than the naive TFM at every registered level (by up to \val{s1-noise4-naive-diff} AUC;
\cref{app:secondary}). A single chain is not clearly better than untuned tree-based
self-training, and the registered configuration that is costs up to \val{s3x-cost-nchains-max} chains and a tuned
learner; one chain per TFM started from the tuned learner did as well in our exploratory analysis.
