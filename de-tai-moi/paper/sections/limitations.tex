\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}
% Each item traces to the "scope" column of G3/CLAIM_EVIDENCE_MATRIX.md.
\textbf{Dependence on the TFM.} The method inherits its TFM's response to noisy context labels: with TabPFN~v2 it
stalled under heavy ambiguity with many classes. The two TFMs were compared on the same data only in Study~1 and in the
response-map analysis, so the cross-study contrast is not a controlled comparison of TFMs.
\textbf{Theory versus the default sampler.} Our guarantees cover the single-site, swap and MH-corrected samplers; the
uncorrected block samplers used by default have none that is non-vacuous. The exact sampler did not predict better overall,
but it mixes more slowly, so the bias of the approximation has not been isolated on real data.
Tables have at most 10{,}000 rows, and contexts above 2{,}000 points are subsampled (\val{s2-ctxsub-n} of
\val{s2-ndata} datasets in Study~2), an approximation \cref{thm:stability} covers only through $\bar\varepsilon$, not isolated.
\textbf{Mixing.} Thirty sweeps do not give draws from a converged posterior over individual labels, and with one chain
per seed the PLL test of Study~2 at $q=0.6$ is sensitive to Monte Carlo variation (\cref{sec:ablation}).
\textbf{Scope.} The claims cover LLP with bags of 8 and PLL. As \cref{prop:naive} predicts, the sampler did not improve on
the naive TFM for PU with labels selected completely at random, class-dependent noise or semi-supervised learning, and
it was not significantly better than the primary comparator for bags of 16 or 32 (Study~1). On PLL the gain over tuned CC
is about one accuracy point, close to the oracle ceiling.
