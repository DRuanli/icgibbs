\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}
% Each item traces to the "scope" column of G3/CLAIM_EVIDENCE_MATRIX.md.
\paragraph{The TFM decides.} The same sampler did not beat the strongest specialised baselines with TabPFN~v2 (Study~1)
and did with TabICL~v2 (Study~2). The two TFMs were compared on the same data only in Study~1, where TabICL~v2 was an
ablation, and in the exploratory response-map analysis; the studies use different datasets, so they do not form a
controlled comparison of TFMs. We do not claim the method works with any TFM; practitioners should check the response of
their TFM to noisy context labels (\cref{fig:mechanism}) before relying on it.

\paragraph{Theory and the default sampler.} Our guarantees cover the single-site, swap and MH-corrected samplers. The
uncorrected block and bag-block samplers used by default have no non-vacuous guarantee (\cref{prop:blocks}); the exact
variant does not predict better at the same budget, but it also mixes more slowly, so the bias of the approximation
itself has not been measured on real data. Exactness also holds only
with respect to the TFM's prior, and the exact transductive predictor was worse than the inductive one.

\paragraph{Mixing.} Thirty sweeps give predictions that are stable across chains, but label-level $\widehat R$ shows that the
chains have not mixed over individual labels, so the samples should not be read as draws from a converged posterior.

\paragraph{Scope and size of the gains.} The claims cover LLP with bags of 8 and PLL. In Study~1, \ours{} did not improve
on the naive TFM for PU with labels selected completely at random, class-dependent noise or semi-supervised learning,
as \cref{prop:naive} predicts, and it was not significantly better than the primary comparator for bags of 16 or 32. On PLL the gain over tuned CC is about one
accuracy point, close to an oracle ceiling. Datasets have at most 10{,}000 rows and contexts at most 2{,}000 points; one
baseline (default PRODEN) failed deterministically on one Study~2 dataset.
