\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}
\textbf{What each study supports.} The comparison with specialised baselines is confirmatory only for the single chain
(Studies~1 and~2), and on PLL its margin is small. Study~3 has \val{s3-n-total} datasets (\val{s3-n-multi} multiclass)
and registered one baseline, the tuned learner $L$; its comparison with $L$ is exploratory. The non-neural baselines of
\cref{sec:s3b} were registered after Study~3 was scored and run untuned; tuned tree-based learners might close part of
the gap. The typical gain of several starts is small (median \val{s3-h1-median}) and came from the learner start, which
needs a trained learner.
\textbf{Failed criteria and predictions.} We have no label-free diagnostic of stalling that transfers across TFMs; with
one TFM the multi-start sampler was more than 0.02 behind the tuned learner in \val{s3-c4-vs-L} of \val{s3-n-pairs}
pairs; and our prediction that longer chains would barely improve (P22) was false.
\textbf{Theory versus practice.} The exactness results do not cover the uncorrected blocks used by default.
\Cref{thm:basins} covers the default sampler but not LLP or subsampled contexts, rests on an assumption we could test
only through its consequences along chains, with maps that need true labels, is not quantitative at our sample sizes,
and does not describe slow mixing, which longer chains showed to be part of what stalls a chain.
\textbf{Scope and cost.} Weak labels are synthetic except for three PLL datasets, and TFM developers may have used some
of our datasets (we excluded only TabDPT's pretraining sets). The claims cover LLP with random bags of 8 and PLL with
uniform distractors at $q\le0.6$; the sampler did not improve on the naive TFM for PU, label noise or semi-supervised
learning (\cref{app:secondary}). A single chain is not clearly better than untuned tree-based self-training, and the
configuration that is costs up to twelve chains and a tuned learner.
