\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}
% Each item traces to the "scope" column of G3/CLAIM_EVIDENCE_MATRIX.md.
\textbf{What each study supports.} The comparison with specialised baselines is confirmatory only for the single chain
(Studies~1 and~2), and on PLL its margin is small. Study~3 has \val{s3-n-total} datasets (\val{s3-n-multi} multiclass)
and one baseline, the tuned learner $L$; its comparison with $L$ is exploratory. The typical gain of several starts is
small (median \val{s3-h1-median}) and came from the learner start, which needs a trained learner.
\textbf{Failed criteria.} We have no label-free diagnostic of stalling that transfers across TFMs, and with one TFM the
multi-start sampler was more than 0.02 behind the tuned learner in \val{s3-c4-vs-L} of \val{s3-n-pairs} pairs.
\textbf{Theory versus practice.} The exactness results do not cover the uncorrected blocks used by default. \Cref{thm:basins} covers the default sampler but not LLP or
subsampled contexts, rests on an assumption we could test only through its consequences along chains, with maps that
need true labels, and is not quantitative at our sample sizes.
\textbf{Scope and cost.} Weak labels are synthetic except for three PLL datasets, the baselines are neural learners,
and TFM developers may have used some of our datasets (we excluded only TabDPT's pretraining sets). The claims
cover LLP with bags of 8 and PLL with uniform distractors; the sampler did not improve on the naive TFM for PU, label
noise or semi-supervised learning (\cref{app:secondary}). The multi-TFM average costs up to twelve chains.
