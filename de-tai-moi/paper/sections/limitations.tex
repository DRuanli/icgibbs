\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}
% Each item traces to the "scope" column of G3/CLAIM_EVIDENCE_MATRIX.md.
\textbf{What each study supports.} The comparison with specialised baselines is confirmatory only for the single chain
(Studies~1 and~2). Study~3 has \val{s3-n-total} datasets (\val{s3-n-multi} multiclass) and one baseline, the tuned
learner $L$, and its comparison with $L$ is exploratory. The typical gain of several starts is small (median
\val{s3-h1-median}) and came from the learner start, which requires training a specialised learner.
\textbf{Failed criteria.} We have no label-free diagnostic of stalling that transfers across TFMs, and with one TFM the
multi-start sampler fell behind a tuned learner in \val{s3-c4-rate} of cases.
\textbf{TFMs.} TFM developers may have used some of our public datasets; we excluded TabDPT's pretraining sets from
Study~3 but cannot check the others.
\textbf{Theory versus practice.} The exactness results do not cover the uncorrected blocks used by default, for which
\cref{prop:predbias} is exact in one conjugate model only. \Cref{thm:basins} covers the default sampler but not LLP or
subsampled contexts, its constants are loose, and its assumption was checked with maps that need true labels.
\textbf{Scope and cost.} The claims cover LLP with bags of 8 and PLL with uniformly drawn distractors. The sampler did
not improve on the naive TFM for PU, label noise or semi-supervised learning, nor significantly on the primary
comparator for bags of 16 or 32; in development runs bags of 32 and instance-dependent candidate sets were hard for
every TFM (\cref{app:dev}). The four-TFM average costs twelve chains.
