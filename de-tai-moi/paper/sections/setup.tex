\section{Problem setup}\label{sec:setup}
% Source: G4/PROOFS.md §0, G4/METHOD_SPEC.md §1, §3.

\paragraph{Data and prior.} We observe features $X=(x_1,\dots,x_n)$ whose labels $y=(y_1,\dots,y_n)\in[C]^n$ are
latent. As in the prior-fitted networks that underlie tabular foundation models \citep{muller2022pfn}, we assume
an exchangeable prior: a latent mechanism $\phi\sim p(\phi)$ and $(x_i,y_i)\overset{\text{iid}}{\sim}p(x,y\mid\phi)$,
with a test pair $(x_*,y_*)$ drawn from the same mechanism. For a labelled context $D=\{(x_j,y_j)\}_j$, the
\emph{posterior predictive distribution} (PPD) of the prior is $p(y_*\mid x_*,D)$; a TFM returns an approximation
$\tfm(y_*\mid x_*,D)$ in a single forward pass.

\paragraph{Weak labels.} Instead of $y$ we observe $\W\sim p(\W\mid X,y)$, independent of $(x_*,y_*)$ given
$(X,y)$ (assumption A0), through a known observation model $\lik(y)=p(\W\mid X,y)$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Partial labels (PLL):} each $x_i$ comes with a candidate set $S_i\ni y_i$, and
        $\lik(y)=\prod_i\mathbb 1[y_i\in S_i]$ (the standard assumption that the probability of a candidate set does
        not depend on which of its members is the true label).
  \item \emph{Label proportions (LLP):} the points are split into disjoint bags $B_1,\dots,B_G$ and only the label
        counts $w_g$ of each bag are observed: $\lik(y)=\prod_g\mathbb 1[\mathrm{counts}(y_{B_g})=w_g]$.
\end{itemize}
Positive-unlabelled data, class-dependent label noise and semi-supervised learning are further instance-level
cases ($\lik(y)=\prod_i\lik_i(y_i)$); Study~1 evaluated them as secondary settings (\cref{app:secondary}).

\paragraph{Goal.} The target of inference is the posterior over latent labels and the resulting predictive,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target}
  \pi(y)=p(y\mid X,\W)\propto p(y\mid X)\,\lik(y),\qquad p(y_*\mid x_*,X,\W)=\sum_y p(y\mid x_*,X,\W)\,p(y_*\mid x_*,(X,y)).
\end{equation}
Neither factor of $\pi$ is available in closed form for a TFM's prior, and no weight of the TFM may be updated.
Our analysis uses three assumptions: (A1) the TFM is exact, $\tfm=\ppd$, on every context the algorithm queries;
(A2) the prior's PPD is positive; (A3) $\lik$ is known and $\sum_y p(y\mid X)\lik(y)>0$. Theorem~\ref{thm:stability}
removes (A1).
